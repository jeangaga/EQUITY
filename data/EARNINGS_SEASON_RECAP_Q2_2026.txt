INDEX-LEVEL EARNINGS PICTURE

Q2 2026 is a genuinely strong U.S. earnings season, but the headline index growth rate materially overstates recurring operating growth. FactSet's August 7 snapshot, with 88% of the S&P 500 reported, showed blended EPS growth of 50.4%, 86% of companies beating EPS and 76% beating revenue; excluding Alphabet and Amazon, EPS growth was still 32.0% and the earnings surprise was 10.9%, both comfortably above normal. FactSet's August 10 update put blended revenue growth at 15.0%, the strongest since Q4 2021, with all eleven sectors reporting year-over-year revenue growth. Reuters/LSEG's August 20 late-season read was similar: roughly 52% aggregate earnings growth, but about 33% once the large AI-equity valuation gains at Alphabet and Amazon were removed. The accounting caveat is large: Alphabet's Q2 GAAP result included about $98B of net unrealized equity gains, while Amazon reported $53.4B of non-operating pretax other income, primarily related to Anthropic. FactSet's last directly verified margin snapshot on July 28 showed a record 15.7% blended S&P 500 net margin, falling to 14.4% excluding Alphabet; the margin record therefore cannot be treated as a clean broad-based operating step-up. Forward guidance has not collapsed: late-season Q3 earnings expectations remained very strong, but exact positive-versus-negative preannouncement counts are not re-verified here. Bottom line: the headline is distorted, the underlying season is still unusually strong.

US GROWTH

The earnings tape is healthier than the macro narrative implied going into the season. The cleanest signals come from capital formation and corporate activity rather than from a uniform consumer boom. JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley showed exceptional trading, underwriting and advisory activity; BlackRock delivered strong organic flows; Wells Fargo showed post-asset-cap balance-sheet expansion; Caterpillar linked AI and infrastructure demand to physical equipment; and GE Aerospace continued to convert backlog and installed-base demand into cash. The human tape is broadly consistent with this: the feared earnings recession did not arrive, but investors are increasingly trading acceleration and guidance quality rather than merely strong absolute growth.

The caveat is concentration. AI infrastructure, energy and capital-markets activity are doing disproportionate work, while parts of household discretionary demand are softer. The better macro formulation is selective resilience: enterprise technology, capital markets, infrastructure, aerospace and power are strong; lower-income discretionary demand is under more pressure. That is supportive for activity, but it is not a broad reflation signal.

US CONSUMER

The consumer is K-shaped rather than broadly broken. Visa and Mastercard reported healthy payment and cross-border activity; Airbnb showed resilient premium and experiential demand; Amazon and Shopify supported a constructive e-commerce read; and Apple still showed high-end device demand despite cost and accounting caveats. Against that, McDonald's provided the clearest supplied-block evidence of pressure at the lower end, with weaker traffic and more skipped restaurant occasions. External mass-retail commentary during the late season pointed in the same direction: the lower end is becoming more selective even when aggregate nominal spending remains healthy.

The implication is that purchasing power and corporate pricing power matter more than the aggregate consumer headline. Premium travel, high-income consumption and digital commerce remain resilient; value-sensitive discretionary categories face more frequent trade-down, fewer occasions and tougher price elasticity. The tape supports a K-shaped consumer, but the lower leg is becoming more relevant to the macro debate.

FINANCIAL CONDITIONS / CREDIT

Credit remains broadly controlled in the supplied bank tape. JPMorgan lowered its card net-charge-off outlook, Bank of America reported improving credit metrics, Wells Fargo expanded loans and deposits without a visible loss deterioration, and MercadoLibre combined very rapid credit growth with strong asset quality. This does not rule out pockets of stress, but it argues against a broad household or corporate-credit break at the point of Q2 reporting.

Capital markets have reopened, but the more disciplined description is "reopened with event concentration" rather than the start of a guaranteed multi-year investment-banking boom. Goldman, JPMorgan and Morgan Stanley all showed strong trading and fee activity, and Morgan Stanley converted IPO events into recurring wealth assets. BlackRock's flows reinforce the financial-conditions signal. The next test is whether backlogs and pipelines convert after the unusually active quarter rather than simply whether Q2 revenue annualizes.

AI / CAPEX / INFRASTRUCTURE

The central AI question has moved from "is demand real?" to "who can show the revenue, margin and cash-flow path from the spend?" The supplied tape answers the first question clearly: demand is real and broadening. Microsoft gave the strongest capex-conversion evidence, with Azure accelerating to 43%, commercial RPO reaching $678B and further Azure acceleration guided despite supply constraints. Amazon provided a second strong case: AWS growth re-accelerated, operating margins expanded and backlog reached $496B, allowing investors to tolerate an exceptionally large capex programme despite free-cash-flow pressure.

The market is not giving every spender the same benefit of the doubt. Meta's advertising franchise improved, but capex absorbed nearly all quarterly operating cash flow and FCF fell sharply; the company has less backlog-style evidence than Microsoft or AWS. TSMC raised the demand and revenue trajectory but also raised the capital burden, shifting the stock debate toward free cash flow, depreciation and overseas-fab dilution. Tesla remains the weakest conversion case in the supplied set: capital spending and autonomy/robotics ambition are rising while consolidated operating economics and cash conversion remain much less proven.

The next constraint is increasingly physical. Micron shows that memory scarcity is transmitting into extraordinary supplier economics, while Amazon and Apple show the downstream cost side of the same cycle. ASML, ASM International and Applied Materials point to continued equipment intensity; Caterpillar, Vistra, Schneider Electric, Siemens Energy and Rolls-Royce show AI demand reaching construction, grid equipment, turbines and power systems. The voice tape increasingly treats power availability, interconnection and long-lead electrical equipment as a first-order constraint alongside memory and advanced packaging. That broadens the opportunity set, but it also raises the effective cost and time-to-revenue of new compute capacity.

EUROPE

Europe's Q2 earnings season is materially better than its macro reputation, but the improvement is sector-led rather than a broad consumer revival. Reuters/LSEG's August 19 tracker put STOXX 600 earnings growth at 24.1%, with Energy up 138.6%, but earnings still growing 13.1% excluding Energy; revenue growth was 11.2%. FactSet's August 14 snapshot, with roughly 83% of companies reported, showed about 20% earnings growth, 9.5% sales growth and 65% of estimate revisions moving upward. The caveat is that upside magnitude has been concentrated: early-season FactSet/Goldman work showed fewer companies than normal beating by more than 5%, and Consumer Discretionary remained the clear weak cohort.

European banks are a genuine positive cohort. Santander, BNP Paribas, UniCredit, UBS and HSBC all showed resilient earnings despite very different rate and capital structures. NII has not simply collapsed with the easing cycle, while capital-markets and wealth activity remain strong. The debate has shifted from immediate earnings delivery toward regulation, capital deployment, M&A and cost discipline.

Industrials, defense and power infrastructure are the cleanest European leadership group. Airbus and Safran are converting aerospace demand into deliveries and aftermarket earnings; Rolls-Royce has broadened its turnaround into defense and power systems; Rheinmetall shows rearmament converting into sales, profit and backlog; Schneider Electric and Siemens Energy provide direct evidence that data-center power, grids and electrification are entering reported orders and margins. The constraint is increasingly execution, supply capacity, working capital and interconnection rather than weak demand.

Technology is also stronger than a simple "Europe lacks tech" framing suggests. ASML and ASM International remain direct beneficiaries of advanced-node, HBM and equipment intensity, while SAP shows resilient mission-critical cloud and ERP demand. The consumer side is much more uneven. Richemont is the cleanest hard-luxury signal; Kering shows early turnaround evidence; LVMH is stabilizing rather than fully reaccelerating; Hermès remains resilient but faces a very high expectation bar. L'Oréal and Unilever show better underlying demand than the luxury weak spots, while Nestlé's volume recovery has not yet translated cleanly into earnings. The right human framing is: Europe finally has a credible earnings-leadership cohort, but not yet a broad-based revival.

ASIA

No broad Japan stock blocks were supplied, so the Japan read remains deliberately external and brief. Reuters Breakingviews on August 10 reported that Japanese listed-company pretax profits were up nearly 50%, with roughly one-third of that increase linked to yen weakness; 14.5% of companies had raised full-year guidance versus about 5% a year earlier, while year-to-date buybacks had already exceeded ¥20T. The important structural read is that earnings improvement is broadening into banking, gaming, pricing power, governance and capital return rather than being only a currency translation story.

The yen is still the key caveat. A stronger currency would remove part of the export translation benefit and raise the hurdle for future earnings beats. Japan therefore looks structurally better than a pure weak-yen trade, but the earnings story is not yet independent of FX.

Within the supplied EM blocks, Taiwan is represented by TSMC, where AI and advanced-node demand remain exceptional but capital intensity is the valuation battleground. Latin America is represented most directly by MercadoLibre and Santander's regional exposure, both of which show selective resilience rather than a broad EM macro conclusion. HSBC and European luxury provide indirect China/Hong Kong read-throughs. No broader EM earnings claim is warranted from the supplied evidence.

CROSS-ASSET PM TAKE

The season remains supportive for equities, but the market is no longer rewarding strong earnings mechanically. The human tape is consistent across strategists, journalists and high-engagement market commentary: guidance quality, cash conversion, capital discipline and the second derivative of growth now matter more than the size of the historical beat. Microsoft and Amazon were rewarded because they paired high capex with visible revenue conversion and contracted demand; several other AI and growth names showed that strong current fundamentals can still be sold when the forward hurdle is already extreme.

This matters for rates. The AI build is increasingly capital-intensive and physically constrained, which makes long-duration earnings more sensitive to financing costs, depreciation, power availability and delivery timing. The same capex wave that supports semiconductors, power and industrials can become a valuation headwind when the incremental dollar of spending does not produce a sufficiently visible revenue or FCF path.

Regionally, the earnings tape supports more breadth than a pure U.S. mega-cap framing. Europe now has a credible banks / aerospace / defense / power / semicap leadership cohort, while Japan has a real governance and capital-return layer alongside its FX sensitivity. That does not remove U.S. leadership, but it reduces the case for treating every non-U.S. market as merely a valuation trade.

BOTTOM LINE

Q2 2026 is a strong earnings season with an unusually important quality adjustment. The U.S. headline is inflated by very large non-operating AI-equity gains, but underlying earnings growth, revenue growth and beat breadth remain genuinely strong after those effects are stripped out. The earnings recession did not arrive; the hurdle for what counts as a good print has simply risen.

The strongest U.S. macro signals are an open capital-markets channel, controlled credit, resilient enterprise and infrastructure demand, and a K-shaped rather than broken consumer. The main new risk is that AI capital intensity is rising faster than cash conversion at several highly valued companies, while memory, power and financing costs increasingly determine the economics of the build.

Europe is delivering a real earnings improvement led by banks, energy, aerospace, defense, power infrastructure and selected technology, but the consumer remains the weak link. Japan's earnings picture is also improving structurally through buybacks, governance and domestic earnings, although the yen is still a large part of the near-term P&L.

For a PM, the season's central question is no longer "are earnings good?" It is "which earnings are recurring, cash-converting and supported by visible demand relative to the capital required to produce them?" The market is rewarding visibility over optionality, acceleration over merely strong growth, and credible FCF paths over spend alone.

As of 2026-08-26, NVIDIA's Q2 release is not included in the supplied stock-block set and remains a final arbitration point for the accelerator layer of the AI thesis.

THEME: Index-level strength survives the one-off adjustment
COMMENTARY: The S&P 500 headline is unusually distorted by Alphabet and Amazon valuation gains, but the underlying season is still strong. FactSet had 50.4% blended growth at its August 7 snapshot versus 32.0% excluding the two companies, with 86% EPS beats and 76% revenue beats; revenue growth subsequently reached 15.0%. JPM, GS, MSFT, AMZN, CAT and ABT reinforce the broader operating strength. The correct conclusion is not "clean record earnings" but "very strong earnings with an unusually large accounting-quality caveat."

THEME: AI capex has moved from demand proof to ROI discrimination
COMMENTARY: MSFT and AMZN provide the strongest supplied-block evidence that very high capex can still earn a rerating when recognized revenue, margins and contracted demand accelerate together. META and TSM show the more contested version, where the demand signal is strong but FCF or capital intensity creates a higher proof burden. TSLA remains the boundary case where long-duration AI/autonomy optionality is much less supported by current cash economics. The market is increasingly asking for a break-even and FCF path rather than rewarding spend itself.

THEME: Power, memory and physical delivery are becoming first-order AI constraints
COMMENTARY: MU shows memory scarcity translating into exceptional supplier economics, while AMZN and AAPL show that the same scarcity raises downstream system costs. ASML, ASM.AS and AMAT confirm continuing equipment intensity; CAT, VST, SU.PA, ENR.DE and RR.L show the AI build moving into construction, generation, grid equipment and power systems. The bottleneck is broadening from chips toward power availability, interconnection, advanced packaging and long-lead electrical equipment, which both extends the beneficiary set and raises time-to-revenue risk.

THEME: AI demand and AI-equity marks are not fully independent
COMMENTARY: The season's largest accounting distortions and some of its strongest infrastructure demand signals are linked to the same private model-lab ecosystem. AMZN's large non-operating gain was primarily Anthropic-related, while MSFT also carried AI-investment effects; Alphabet's external index-level result adds the same issue outside the supplied block set. This means index EPS quality, hyperscaler backlog quality and private AI valuations can share common counterparties. The risk is correlation rather than a claim that the demand itself is artificial.

THEME: Capital markets have reopened, with event concentration
COMMENTARY: GS, JPM and MS delivered exceptional trading, underwriting and advisory activity, while MS showed that IPO events can convert into recurring Wealth Management assets. BLK's flows reinforce the risk-appetite signal. The caveat is repeatability: major transactions and elevated volatility contributed materially, so the next proof point is backlog and pipeline conversion rather than annualizing Q2 revenue.

THEME: The consumer is K-shaped, and the lower leg is becoming more visible
COMMENTARY: V, MA, ABNB, AMZN and SHOP show resilient aggregate, premium, travel and digital demand. MCD is the clearest supplied-block warning, with negative traffic and more skipped occasions. AAPL is better treated as a pricing-power and premium-demand datapoint than as proof of broad consumer strength because cost and accounting effects complicate the quarter. The macro read is selective resilience, not a broad consumer break.

THEME: The market is trading the second derivative
COMMENTARY: Strong fundamentals did not guarantee a positive share reaction. TSM, AMD, AMAT, GE, NVO and several European leaders show that a beat can be sold when the setup already discounts persistent acceleration, while MSFT, AMZN, SHOP, ABT and GS were rewarded when the print changed the forward debate. Guidance quality, acceleration, positioning and cash conversion increasingly matter more than the historical beat itself.

THEME: Europe finally has a credible earnings-leadership cohort
COMMENTARY: SAN.MC, BNP.PA, UBSG.SW, HSBA.L and UCG.MI show resilient European financials; AIR.PA, SAF.PA, RR.L, RHM.DE, SU.PA and ENR.DE show strong industrial, defense and power conversion; ASML, ASM.AS and SAP.DE show high-quality technology demand. Aggregate earnings growth is strong even ex-Energy, but Consumer Discretionary remains weak and beat magnitude is less broad than the index headline implies. Europe is improving enough to matter for relative performance, but the recovery is still sector-led.

THEME: Bank earnings are healthy, but the rate path and return framework still matter
COMMENTARY: JPM and BAC offered constructive NII framing, WFC showed strong balance-sheet growth without a formal NII raise, and C produced better current earnings without lifting its return target. In Europe, SAN.MC, BNP.PA and HSBA.L showed NII resilience under a different policy mix, while UBSG.SW and UCG.MI face more capital and strategic constraints. Credit remains controlled, but the group is not homogeneous enough to support a simple "banks win from higher rates" conclusion.

THEME: True reversals are selective, not complete
COMMENTARY: UNH moved from repeated forecast failure to a raised earnings and cash-flow framework, but reserve development and membership selection still matter. KER.PA has moved from hope to early evidence as Gucci stabilizes, WFC is showing post-asset-cap growth in reported numbers, and NESN.SW is recovering volumes without yet proving full earnings conversion. These are improving states rather than completed turnarounds.

THEME: Geopolitics and policy are direct P&L variables
COMMENTARY: TTE.PA shows Middle East disruption reducing production while improving crude and refining economics. AAPL's tariff refunds affected margins, ASML and the equipment chain remain exposed to export controls, and HSBA.L carries direct China/Hong Kong policy and real-estate sensitivities. European defense demand and steel policy are also visible in RHM.DE and MT.AS. Geopolitics is entering earnings through volumes, pricing, taxes, capital rules and order books rather than only through risk premia.

THEME: Visibility and disclosure quality are becoming valuation inputs
COMMENTARY: MSFT's RPO, AMZN's backlog and the long-dated order books at ASML, aerospace and power names provide investors with instruments to underwrite future revenue. NFLX moved in the opposite direction by reducing engagement disclosure at the same time forward growth slowed, creating an information-quality discount. META's capex story is also judged partly by the absence of a backlog-style measure. The market is paying more for verifiable duration and charging more for opacity.
